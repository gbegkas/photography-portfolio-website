I've been to the UK more times than I can count over the last five years, 
always for work, passing through London each time without ever really stopping. I'd 
wanted to visit properly for a couple of years now, ever since my photography 
started improving. This time I finally planned a weekend before my commitments began,
and as always I brought my camera along.

I spent the weekend walking through the familiar sights: St James's Park, Buckingham 
Palace, Trafalgar Square, Big Ben, the London Eye, before heading out to Greenwich 
Park. The streets were busy, as any weekend in central London tends to be, and that 
turned out to be the real challenge. More than once I'd see something worth 
photographing, a stranger's expression, a small interaction, a moment that would 
have made a great frame, and hesitate. Photographing people without their consent 
still doesn't sit entirely comfortably with me, and there's always that small worry
at the back of my mind of being noticed, or worse, confronted. Street photography is
something I'm still working on, not just technically, but in finding the confidence 
to actually raise the camera.

Because of that, I came home with fewer photographs I loved than I'd hoped. But 
there were plenty of moments where I slowed down without meaning to, standing in 
front of a railing or a shopfront longer than made sense, waiting for the light 
or the angle to feel right, paying more attention to perspective, detail and 
composition than to the landmarks themselves.

London is an easy city to photograph. It's full of instantly recognisable 
subjects, which is exactly what makes it hard to find something that feels 
personal. I'm not sure I quite managed that this time, but that's part of the 
fun. Every trip is another chance to experiment, and to get a little more 
comfortable photographing places, and people, outside my comfort zone.

Given how often work brings me back to the UK, I suspect London will give me 
plenty more chances to try.

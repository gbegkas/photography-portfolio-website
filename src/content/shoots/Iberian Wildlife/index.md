In late November 2025, I visited Spain for the first time, which was also the first time I tried wildlife photography in
a hide. I was part of a five-person group that included a professional wildlife photographer. The goal was to capture 
the Iberian Lynx and the local bird species. 

Right from the start, I faced the hard truth of wildlife photography: nothing guarantees you will achieve your goals. 
We spent two days from dawn till dusk in a hide in southeastern Spain near the border of Castilla-La Mancha and Andalucia where the lynx lives. 

We only saw one lynx in the early hours of the
first day, while it was still misty from the night and the sun had not yet risen. This single appearance lasted only a 
couple of minutes — though it felt like mere seconds. The animal emerged from the left, crossed our field of view at an 
unhurried pace, and disappeared into the trees and undergrowth without so much as a pause. 

The rest of the time we had the opportunity to capture the diverse bird community of the area. On the third day, we 
visited a hide west of Madrid where we had the opportunity to see and capture the Spanish Imperial Eagle. Again we 
weren't that fortunate, as the only appearance of the eagle happened early in the morning when it was still cold and 
humid from the night and the hide glass had fogged up from our body heat. The day, though, was amazing. 

Magpies, ravens, red foxes, red kites, and of course the Imperial Eagle were just a few of the 
species I had the opportunity to observe and capture with my lens — but the most unbelievable moment was when a group of
around 30–40 black and Egyptian vultures came down all at once in front of the hide.

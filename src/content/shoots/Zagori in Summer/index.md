title: Zagori in Summer
description: 'Three days among stone villages, mountain landscapes and very late nights under the stars'
date: 2026-07-15
---

In early July, I headed to Zagori in northwestern Greece for a 
three-day trip built mainly around astrophotography. The plan was 
to spend the nights under the stars and use the days to explore 
the region and photograph its landscapes — although, after a few 
very late nights, our idea of “exploring” became somewhat slower 
than usual.

We moved between villages, taking in Kato Pedina, Aristi, Vikos,
Papingo, Konitsa, Monodendri and Fragades along the way. Zagori 
is one of those places where it is difficult to go very far without 
stopping to look. The landscape changes constantly, with deep 
gorges, steep mountains and villages seemingly tucked into every
available corner of the terrain.

The villages themselves are also a big part of what makes Zagori 
so distinctive. The houses are traditionally built from the local
stone, with stone-tiled roofs that give the villages a very 
characteristic appearance. The same stonework continues throughout
the region, with old stone arch bridges connecting paths and roads
across the landscape. It gives the villages and the surrounding 
landscape a sense of continuity that I found really interesting to
photograph.

Of course, the conditions weren't quite what I had imagined. It
was hotter than I expected, the summer sun was intense, and many 
of the rivers and streams were running low. The middle of summer 
probably isn't the best time to see Zagori at its most dramatic, 
especially if you're looking for the lush greens and flowing water
that make the region so recognisable.

But I actually enjoyed working with what we had. The strong light
wasn't always forgiving, but it also created some interesting 
contrasts across the mountains and gorges. And after spending the 
nights looking up at the sky, there was something nice about 
slowing down during the day and simply taking in the landscape.

What I enjoyed most about the trip was probably the feeling of 
constantly moving through the landscape. One village would open 
onto a completely different view from the last, and even the drives
between places became part of the experience. Sometimes we stopped
because there was a photograph worth taking; other times we just 
stopped because the view was too good to drive past.

The final collection isn't quite the grand summer portrait of 
Zagori I had imagined before the trip. The conditions weren't 
perfect, and I came back with fewer landscape photographs than 
I expected. But there are still a few images that bring me 
straight back to those three days — the mountains, the gorges, 
the stone villages and the quiet roads between them.
